    Brackwater Verge is a cluster of low islands in a shallow sea, where the tides run higher and wider than anywhere else on the inner sea. Land and water never quite agree on where one ends and the other begins. Rivers become roads for a few hours a day. Old roads lie three fathoms under water on a flood morning. Tidal flats turn to sea and back again with every tide, and the shifting sand uncovers ruins where there were none last year. There is nothing magical in it: only ebb and flood. Verge is the least magical land in Orvessa and the one most like an ordinary mortal country, with fishing towns, farms, markets and fog. That is why, when something is wrong here, it shows. The mood is cosy, and a little gothic, and a little off.

    The restless tides

    The fishermen of Verge have a tale for it. Before the Last Shore, they say, Seraveth the Tidemother rested here, in the lagoon where Kaelmor now stands, and promised her sea she would come back. She never did, and her tides still wait for her, running high and pulling at the land as if searching for something. Old fishermen call it the Tidemother's grief. The tide-reader at Tidegauge says it is only the shape of the shallows. No one can prove either of them wrong.

    Varnhalt came to Verge to die. Kaelmor was his mother's town, a fishing village then, and the place where his sister-in-arms last stood in peace. Poisoned by a black root, he lay three centuries in his sickbed there, and while he lived, his three watchfires on the heights kept the Deep from rising through the islands' caves. They went dark the night he died.

    Two islands, two moods. Merefold, the ring around the Mere and Kaelmor, is the gentle first island: farms, fishing villages and small troubles, close to the city's walls. Ebbholm, the island to the east, is far harder: heavily invaded and partly occupied by the dark. The player steps from one to the other at around level 5, and the difference should be felt at once.

    First steps

    Mudlings are the first enemies: small crawling things of mud and bone that come up out of the tide pools around the lagoon. They frighten fishermen more than they hurt them. Something a little off, not yet horror.

    Old Hennick, the dock worker who came back from the fog not himself, is the first Hollowed the player sees. He vanishes after a few days, and returns later as crew on the Blackwake.

    Maudry Saltgrave runs the smokehouse in the fishing village on the Netless Coast and gives the player their first simple tasks. Warm and suspicious, she keeps her own list of the fishermen who did not come home. Her husband's name is at the top.

    The dark comes to Verge

    When Varnhalt died, the chains on Ezrakhar broke, and the dark legions began to crawl up from below to take the dead king's place. In Verge they come two ways: up through the caves, the mines and the dungeon, and in from the sea on the dead ships. This is what the player fights from level 1 to 10.

    How much the player sees of Ezrakhar in Verge. In levels 1–10 the player hears Ezrakhar's name only once, in Varnhalt's last message. Everywhere else in Verge the player meets the Physician, the cult and "the dark", never Ezrakhar by name. The links to Ezrakhar written in this tab are for the designer; the game reveals them in later zones. To the player, the invasion of Verge looks like a threat of its own, with nothing to do with Ezrakhar. Only later does it turn out to be tied to him.

    The Rot. The people of Verge call the invasion the Rot. They think it is a sickness spreading through the land, and most believe it comes from the king's death: his power is gone, so the land sickens. It fits what they saw, since the watchfires went dark the night he died and the Rot began after.

    Two capitals. Kaelmor is the Syndicate's seat of trade and council. The Thousand Keels, the floating city, are where the Syndicate truly lives.

    The dark corrupts every living thing. The Synod and the root do not only raise the dead: animals, beasts and people of every race can be made rot-touched and turned to the dark. In Verge it starts with shore-creatures; later the player meets rot-touched humans and every other people.

    Crowded places

    Crowded places, where the mobs stand close as in a WoW camp:

    • The wreckers' coast on the Netless Coast: three camps of four, and Black Ansel by his false light.
    • The smugglers' holm at the Flats Beacon: tents, crates and patrols.
    • The Neck Village: every house holds rot-touched beasts.
    • The mine camp on the Low Mountain: Hollowed miners in every tent, vein-crawlers in the galleries.
    • The salvagers' dig rings in the lowlands.
    • The cut-throats' palisade at Firmwater.
    • The South Cape beaches at low tide: Blackwake landing parties.
    • The drowned wreckers' house and graves on Shroud Skerry.

    Quests to kill beasts, not just evil: wolves on the Lent Shore, crabs and pool-crawlers, rot-touched boars and dogs in the lowlands, sandworms on the flats, marsh rats in Braidmarsh. No good or harmless animals are ever quest targets.

    Caves and mines

    • The hill mine (levels 5–7). A shaft under timber props, with an ore cart on rails. Vein-crawlers nest in the far galleries.
    • The west cave (level 6). A dark chamber with quartz in its walls, cave crystal and cave moss.
    • The cape mine (level 9). Older and poorer than the hill mine, with an empty cart. Deep-crawlers live below.
    • The lava tunnel of Kilnholm (level 7–9). Slag-crawlers, volcanic glass, and the Seam Matriarch at the end.
    • The cave behind the tide, under the Headland. Open only at low water. Its back wall is a crack that breathes.

    Landmarks

    • The three watchfires. The Headland Beacon, the Flats Beacon and the Ridge Beacon were lit by Varnhalt's own hand and have been tended by the old families of Kaelmor ever since. All three went dark on the night he died. A rekindled fire burns for a while, gutters and goes out. The spire's three lamps in Kaelmor answer them.
    • The standing stones on Anchor Ridge, carved with a sign that will not keep its shape.
    • The anchor hollow, where the pulled rune-stake lies, ringed with a scratched broken chain.
    • The sink on the South Flats, with roofs far below and a sealed hatch. What city lies under Verge is not known.
    • The pillar walk, the drowned old quay of Varnhalt's vassals, whose pillars show at low water.
    • The drowned milestone, readable only on dry mornings.
    • The islet in the river, which only the causeway reaches at ebb. The cut-throats keep their weapons and powder there.
    • The winch loft in Kaelmor, whose door opens onto the water and can be reached only at low tide.

    Travel

    The ship for the mainland sails from Kaelmor's south-west quay to the mainland quay in Port Ossery and back: two ships on one route, a departure from each quay every 12:45, about 12 minutes at sea. The fare, 7 coin, is taken aboard once she is clear of the pier, from heroes of level 10 or more who have finished The First Ship; anyone else is set back on the pier and pays nothing. The voyage is sailed the whole way: the hero walks aboard at the pier while she rests there, stands on her deck at sea and walks off at the far quay.
