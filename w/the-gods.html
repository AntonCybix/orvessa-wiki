    The gods

    The gods of Orvessa, whose deaths or absence still shape the world.

    No god is known to live now. The songs remember dozens; these are the ones whose deaths, or absence, still shape the world. Caedmar alone did not fall: he left, and no one knows whether he lives. Apart from Caedmar, Varnhalt and Seraveth, all names here are working names.

    The gods

    God Domain Where they fell What they left behind
    Caedmar the Far Father Stars, night, guidance; the light that remains when every other light is gone He did not fall. He left the world before the Burning Years His son Varnhalt, the Ilvaren Communion, and Caedmar's Eye, the star that never moves, which sailors steer by
    Varnhalt, the Last Wall (demigod, son of Caedmar) Forge, hearth, the shield wall His hall in Kaelmor, in the Ember Year His watchfires, his runes in the deep places, his statue at Kaelmor, the stolen blade Last Vigil and the spark in every Sparkborn
    Seraveth, the Tidemother Sea, storm, sailors The Last Shore, now Underwake A dry bay where her tides still rise without water. Sailors pour her a drop before every voyage. Her statue faces the sea at Kaelmor.
    Aurelan the Lawgiver Order, oaths, kingship The plain where Varr now stands The oaths of kings and the Ironreach belief in order. The emperors claim his mantle; the Order of the Fallen says they have no right to it.
    Thessa the Greenmother Growth, forests, beasts The heart of Avelorn Rootdeep, the forest that grew from her bones, and the leylines, which the Wildwood call her veins
    Morrow, Keeper of the Last Door Death and passage The field that became Marrowfield Since his fall no one guards the door between life and death. The dead do not always pass on, and the Covenant has learned to use that.
    Velin the Hourkeeper Time and seasons The Pale Reach Broken time where she fell, and the gift of time magic, which only chronomancers still remember her name for
    Kaldren the Stormsower Sky, rain, harvest Storm Orchard Orchards that drink lightning, and the farmers' habit of leaving the last sheaf for a god who no longer comes
    Solvane of the Dawn Sun and light Siltglass He burned himself and a demon host to glass. The desert still shines at noon as if he were looking out of it.

    The gods were not perfect. The songs tell of quarrels, of pride, of gods who arrived late to the war or not at all. But they stood between mortals and the Deep, and now none of them stand there.
