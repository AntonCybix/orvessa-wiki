    Tetherstones and the Homeward Rite

    How the Sparkborn walk home: the game's recall to a chosen inn, about once an hour.

    How the Sparkborn walk home: the game's recall to a chosen inn, about once an hour.

    Description

    The stone

    A tetherstone is a palm-sized river stone that a Runesmith has carved with a single rune and then split clean in two. The two halves are one stone in every way that matters to a rune. Wherever they lie, each half knows where the other one is, and wants to be whole again.

    Whoever buys a tetherstone keeps one half, the carry-half. The other half, the anchor, is left in safekeeping in the strongbox of an inn. That is why a traveller can only ever walk home to one place: a stone has only one other half, and it can only lie in one strongbox.

    The rite

    The Homeward Rite is the rune-craft that turns that longing into a road. The bearer grips the carry-half, speaks the rune, and for a few heartbeats the two halves pull the world between them thin. A portal opens, narrow, cold at the edges and smelling of hot iron, and on the other side is the common room of the inn that holds the anchor. The bearer steps through. The portal closes behind them.

    In the Burning Years, Varnhalt taught the rite to the first innkeepers so that wounded soldiers could be pulled home from the front, and his own power fed every stone. Once he died, that stopped. The stones went dead in a single night, everywhere at once. The only fire left that can wake one is the spark, which is why only the Sparkborn can walk the Homeward Rite in the Ember Year.

    Why only once an hour

    Opening the road burns a little of the bearer's spark. A spark is a god's ember, not a bonfire, and it needs time to rekindle before it can be spent the same way again: about an hour. Runesmiths say that a bearer who forced the rite on a cold spark would arrive without it, and that a Sparkborn with no spark is only a mortal standing in a room full of demons. No one has tried it twice.

    The Innkeepers' Compact

    The strongboxes belong to the Innkeepers' Compact, an old guild of inns sworn to one law: under an inn's roof no blade is drawn and no faction's quarrel is spoken. In return, every power lets the Compact's inns stand. Each Compact inn keeps a strongbox under the hearth, and a ledger of every anchor in it. When a traveller moves their anchor to a new inn, the innkeeper writes it into the ledger and the Compact's runners carry the stone overnight. A traveller who has shown the stone to the new innkeeper can rely on it at once, because the Compact's word is good.

    Inns hold anchors only for those their power is not at war with. A Wildwood inn will not keep an anchor for an Ironreach soldier, and the Covenant's inns are closed to Wildwood stones in the cold peace.

    How it plays

    Rule In the game In the world
    Learning it Paid training at a Runesmiths' hall. The first lesson is in Hallowrise, Kaelmor, early in Verge. The Runesmiths teach the rite for coin; it is their trade.
    The item One tetherstone in the bag, from character creation or bought with the lesson. Cannot be traded. The carry-half of a split stone.
    Choosing the place Talk to any innkeeper in a Compact inn to set it as home. One home at a time. The anchor is handed over and written into the ledger.
    Using it A ten-second cast opens a short-lived portal; the bearer steps through to their home inn. Interrupted by damage. The Homeward Rite.
    Cooldown 60 minutes. The spark has to rekindle.
    Who can travel The bearer only. Group portals can come later, as a Runesmith or Chronomancer talent. One stone opens a road for one spark.
    Upgrades Patron rewards and talents can shorten the cooldown. Better-cut stones, a stronger spark.

    Names (tetherstone, Homeward Rite, the Innkeepers' Compact) are working proposals.
